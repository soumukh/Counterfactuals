# Prompt-Induced Competition Between Context and Parametric Memory in Language Models

Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently encounter conflicts between contextual information provided in prompts and factual knowledge stored in their parametric memory. Understanding how models resolve this competition is essential for developing reliable and controllable AI systems. In this paper, we present a systematic study of prompt-induced context--memory competition using counterfactual question answering. We investigate two complementary factors: (i) meta-prompt regimes that explicitly encourage reliance on contextual information or parametric memory, and (ii) premise-word semantics, where different lexical choices are used to frame identical counterfactual statements. Experiments on seven pretrained and instruction-tuned models from the GPT-2, Qwen2.5, and Llama-3.1 families show that instruction tuning fundamentally reshapes context--memory competition, producing near-complete switching between contextual and factual reasoning under explicit prompts, while pretrained models exhibit substantially weaker transitions. We further show that seemingly minor lexical variations significantly influence model behavior and that a probabilistic competition score provides deeper insight into context--memory competition than answer accuracy alone. These findings improve our understanding of how prompt formulation governs knowledge selection in modern language models and have implications for prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, and reliable LLM reasoning.
